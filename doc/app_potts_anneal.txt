:line

app_style potts/anneal command :h3
app_style potts/anneal/kk command :h3

[Syntax:]

app_style style Q crystal theta_cut keyword values ... :pre

style = {potts/anneal} or {potts/anneal/kk} :ulb,l
Q = labels 1 to Q are the grains of the starting (deformed) structure :l
crystal = {cubic}, {hcp}, {trigonal}, {tetragonal} or {orthorhombic} :l
theta_cut = Read-Shockley cutoff angle (degrees) :l
zero or more keyword/value pairs may be appended :l
keyword = {nucleation} or {recovery} or {mobility} :l
  {nucleation} values = rate Hmin {boundary} {radius} r
    rate = nucleation probability per site per unit time (MCS)
    Hmin = only sites with stored energy >= Hmin nucleate
    {boundary} = optional word: only sites on a grain boundary nucleate
    {radius} r = optional: a nucleus is its seed dilated r times (default 1)
  {recovery} value = r
    r = stored energy decays as dH/dt = -r H
  {mobility} value = theta_m
    theta_m = flips across a boundary of disorientation theta are accepted
              with weight M = 1 - exp(-5 (theta/theta_m)^4) (default: M = 1) :pre
:ule

[Examples:]

app_style potts/anneal 1000000 cubic 15.0 recovery 0.002 nucleation 0.000005 2.0 boundary radius 4
app_style potts/anneal/kk 1000000 cubic 15.0 mobility 15.0 :pre

[Description:]

This is an on-lattice application for heat treatments of a grain
structure in which every site has its own orientation, such as the
output of "app_style additive/texture"_app_additive_texture.html:
recovery, recrystallization and grain growth. Its site values use the
slots of {additive/texture}, so such a state loads with the
"set"_set.html command ({stitch} or {hdf5}) as it is:

i1      = grain label
i2      = site state (5 = void; any other value takes part)
i3      = nucleus seeds of the last sweep (the app's own)
d1..d4  = (q0, qx, qy, qz) unit quaternion
d5      = stored energy H
d6, d7  = unused
d8, d9  = G and V at solidification (read by stored_energy) :pre

Sites of one label belong to one grain and feel no boundary, so the
orientation gradients inside a grain survive. The energy of site I is

Ei = Sum_j E(theta_ij) + Hi :pre

summed over the neighbors J of another label, E the Read-Shockley energy
of their disorientation theta_ij (r (1 - ln r), r = theta/theta_cut,
and 1 at or above theta_cut). A rKMC event at site I picks a random
distinct label among its neighbors; site I would take the label,
orientation and stored energy of a neighbor with that label. The flip
is accepted with probability M min\[1,exp(-dE/kT)\], dE the change in
Ei. Voids never change and lend nothing.

Stored energy enters the same test as the boundary energy, so a
recrystallized grain (H = 0) grows into deformed material by the
stored energy it removes against the boundary it adds. With the
{recovery} keyword H decays everywhere after each sweep.

With the {nucleation} keyword, after each sweep every deformed site
(label <= Q) with H >= Hmin seeds a nucleus with probability rate*dt,
as in "app_style potts/rx"_app_potts_rx.html (with {boundary}, only
sites with a neighbor of another label). The nucleus is grown from its
seed by {radius} dilation passes over deformed, non-void sites, each
joining the neighboring nucleus with the lowest seed ID; it takes label
Q + the seed's site ID, H = 0, and a random orientation drawn from a
table of 65536 (keyed to that ID). The draws do not depend on the
number of processors.

[Stored energy and the Potts temperature:] On sc/26n, run at kT
around 2. At lower temperatures flat boundaries are pinned by the
lattice: a recrystallized grain facets and stops for H between about
1.5 and 4 (kT = 0.5). At kT = 2 a front's velocity is close to linear
in H (within about 10%) for H from about 2 to 8, and boundaries stay
smooth apart from thermal fluctuations. So physical stored energies
are scaled into that window (stored_energy's Hmax). Scaling H down
exaggerates the boundary curvature relative to the stored energy: the
critical nucleus radius, about 22/H sites at kT = 2, is larger than it
is physically, which is what {radius} is for. The app warns when the
stored energy of a deformed site reaches maxneigh-2, above which fronts
grow without regard to curvature and come out rough.

The following additional command is defined by this application:

stored_energy pdas K rho0 theta_c b rho_ref Hmax :pre

It sets H (d5) of every solidified site (i2 = 3) with G > 0 and V > 0
from its primary dendrite arm spacing:

lambda = K G^(-1/2) V^(-1/4)         (Hunt; microns, G in K/m, V in m/s)
rho    = rho0 + theta_c/(b lambda)   (m^-2; theta_c in radians, b in m)
H      = Hmax rho/rho_ref :pre

K = 428.44 is the constant spparks-micro-analysis uses. Other sites
keep their H; set it with "set d5"_set.html.

Time is in Monte Carlo steps (one sweep = 1). The stats output adds a
column Xrx, the recrystallized fraction of the non-void sites.

:line

Style {potts/anneal/kk} is a Kokkos version of the same model, which
runs the sweep, recovery and nucleation in parallel on a GPU or with
OpenMP threads, with "sweep color"_sweep.html or "sweep
color/strict"_sweep.html. With {color/strict}, built without fast math
(as is {potts/anneal}), it gives the same answer as {potts/anneal} bit
for bit on any number of processors. On a GPU, or in a fast-math build,
the disorientation and Read-Shockley energies differ from the host's in
the last bits, so the trajectories differ, with the same statistics;
the GPU result still does not depend on the number of processors.

:line

[Restrictions:]

At most 32 neighbors per site. With {nucleation}, Q + the number of
sites must fit in an int. KMC is not supported.

Style {potts/anneal/kk} is part of the KOKKOS package.  It is only
enabled if SPPARKS was built with that package.  See the "Making
SPPARKS"_Section_start.html#start_3 section for more info.
