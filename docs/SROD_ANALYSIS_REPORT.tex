\author{SURGE-kokkos branch, \code{additive/texture/kk} Moser deck}
\date{Revision 2, 2026-09-09}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Lamb et al.\ measured the starting reference orientation deviation (SROD) of columnar grains in a 3D-EBSD volume of laser powder bed fusion GammaPrint-700 (Scripta Materialia 2024) and, at higher resolution, within grains and cells (Met.\ Trans.\ A 2026). Re-analysing their population curves shows statistics consistent with a random-axis rotation walk, $\mathrm{RMS}^2 = D h$ with $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ against build height and a 5/95 width of about 1.4 times the mean, the Maxwell value; the published curves do not distinguish this from the authors' own two-regime reading over most of the height range, and the mechanism (Lamb: mushy-zone shrinkage and thermomechanical cycling in the heat-affected zone) is not identified by the statistics. Their example grains rotate along a directed path. SPPARKS \code{additive/texture} and \code{additive/texture/kk} carry a phenomenological two-term model applied at capture: a per-voxel walk inherited along each lineage and a coherent per-grain walk of the rotation vector, indexed by inheritance path length and correlated between levels. A Moser-source GP700 deck calibrated on the 2024 originating-grain curve (in-sample RMS \SI{0.34}{\degree}) reproduces a target it was not tuned on, the established-grain curve of the same paper (two builds: RMS 0.28 and \SI{0.47}{\degree}, bias $-0.11$ and \SI{+0.25}{\degree}), which the per-voxel model alone fails (bias \SI{+3.0}{\degree}); against the modal SROD trajectory of the 2026 dataset over its first \SI{120}{\micro\metre} the two models are comparable, the per-voxel model closer below \SI{60}{\micro\metre}. Per grain, the model's accumulation rate (mean \SI{0.84}{\degree} per \SI{30}{\micro\metre}) is half the 2026 average and its extremes fall well short of Lamb's (5 \% of grains above \SI{25}{\degree} against ``most''). Further limitations: the grain structure is locked to the registered hatch pattern of the deck, no surviving grains originate mid-build by nucleation at any density tested (on a \SI{2}{\degree} segmentation the coherent model's intragranular boundaries supply a 55 : 86 originating-to-established ratio against Lamb's $> 500 : 100$), the seed scatter of the population score is about \SI{0.1}{\degree}, and a \SI{2}{\degree} re-segmentation changes the simulated grain population.
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item Population SROD statistics in GP700 are those of a finite-variance rotation walk: mean $\propto \sqrt{h}$ within the resolution of the published curves, $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$, Maxwell width. The same statistics arise from any process with independent increments per unit height, so they constrain the process, not its mechanism. Lamb's example grains (2024 Fig.~4) rotate coherently along a path.
\item Model, both SPPARKS additive apps: \code{misorientation\_diffusion} $D_v$ (per-voxel walk, inherited per hop) plus \code{misorientation\_grain\_walk} $D_g$ $\rho$ \code{path} (coherent per-grain walk, one Gaussian rotation-vector increment per unit of inheritance path length, shared by every voxel of the grain, correlated between levels with persistence $\rho$). Orientation is inherited from one parent voxel and never averaged. The model is an at-capture proxy for what Lamb attribute to post-solidification plasticity.
\item Deck v10: $D_v$ \SI{0.065}{\Dunit}, $D_g$ \SI{0.010}{\Dunit}, $\rho = 0.9$ (correlation length $\approx \SI{30}{\micro\metre}$ of path), calibrated on the 2024 originating curve (in-sample RMS \SI{0.34}{\degree}). Held-out: established curve RMS / bias 0.28 / \SI{-0.11}{\degree} and 0.47 / \SI{+0.25}{\degree} in two builds (v8, per-voxel only: 2.99 / \SI{+2.98}{\degree}); 2026 Fig.~7a mode 1.6 → \SI{4.7}{\degree} over 5--\SI{115}{\micro\metre} against v10's 0.8 → \SI{4.8}{\degree} and v8's 1.8 → \SI{5.8}{\degree}.
\item Per grain (96 filtered grains): drift of the mean orientation median \SI{7.2}{\degree} (v8 \SI{4.2}{\degree}), spread \SI{3.4}{\degree} (v8 \SI{6.1}{\degree}). Against Lamb: accumulation rate mean 0.84 / max \SI{1.92}{\degree} per \SI{30}{\micro\metre} (2026: 1.7 / 5.8); grains with maximum SROD $> \SI{25}{\degree}$: 5 \% (2024: ``most''); per-layer mean at \SI{300}{\micro\metre} median \SI{10.8}{\degree}, max \SI{18}{\degree} (2024 example grains: 20--\SI{25}{\degree}).
\item The constants are per unit of growth path (measured path factor 1.47 at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}, 1.68 at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre}) and first-order resolution independent: with $\rho$ and $D_g$ rescaled for the voxel size the deck reproduces Lamb at the experiment's \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} voxel with a bias of \SI{+0.64}{\degree} (per-voxel term alone: \SI{+0.41}{\degree}); the excess over the \SI{3}{\micro\metre} result grows from 3 \% at \SI{45}{\micro\metre} to 23 \% at \SI{282}{\micro\metre}, and rescaling the constants by the measured path-factor ratio (1.47/1.68) reduces the bias to \SI{+0.18}{\degree}.
\item Limitations: hatch-locked grain structure (boundary autocorrelation 0.6--0.7 at the \SI{80}{\micro\metre} lag; a random per-layer hatch offset leaves the SROD results unchanged and the period only partly reduced); no surviving mid-build origins at nucleation \numrange{6.5e12}{8.5e13} \si{\per\cubic\metre} (0--7 grains per run against Lamb's $> 500$); a \SI{2}{\degree} re-segmentation gives 139 filtered grains of ESD \SI{29}{\micro\metre} (spin labels: 96, \SI{148}{\micro\metre}) with the SROD curve within \SI{0.6}{\degree}; seed scatter of the population score $\approx$\SI{0.05}{\degree} RMS on the proxy and a second full build within \SI{0.3}{\degree}; the \dir{001} fraction within \SI{15}{\degree} of BD is 0.15--0.19 against a random 0.10 and has no measured target.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental methods (Lamb et al.\ 2024, 2026)}
\paragraph{Material and process.} CoNi-base superalloy GammaPrint-700 (SB-CoNi-10), laser powder bed fusion on an SLM Solutions SLM125 at \SI{176}{\watt} and \SI{1137}{\milli\metre\per\second}, \SI{80}{\micro\metre} spot (the papers give the spot size only; the deck reads it as $D4\sigma$), \SI{80}{\micro\metre} hatch, \SI{30}{\micro\metre} layers, bidirectional scan with \SI{90}{\degree} interlayer rotation (the 2024 Fig.~2c sketch shows the direction repeating every two layers and the scan directions at \SI{45}{\degree} to the block edges), single \SI{10}{\milli\metre} raster, \SI{200}{\celsius} plate preheat. The hatch registration between layers of the same direction is not stated. The 2026 paper supplies the thermophysical set used for its 3DThesis simulations: $k = \SI{22.5}{\watt\per\metre\per\kelvin}$ (extrapolated to \SI{950}{\celsius}), $\rho = \SI{8650}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre}$, $c_p = \SI{600}{\joule\per\kilogram\per\kelvin}$ (IN718 at \SI{1000}{\celsius}), liquidus \SI{1373}{\celsius} (\SI{1646}{\kelvin}), and an absorptivity of 0.9 selected to reproduce measured pool dimensions; the solidus, \SI{1319}{\celsius} (\SI{1592}{\kelvin}), is from the 2024 paper.

\paragraph{3D datasets.} 2024: TriBeam tomography (femtosecond-laser serial sectioning with EBSD on each slice), \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} voxels, 385 slices, a $1400 \times 412 \times 477$~\si{\micro\metre} volume with over 9000 grains, reconstructed in DREAM.3D and segmented at a \SI{2}{\degree} misorientation threshold before any SROD calculation. 2026: a $120 \times 120 \times 169$~\si{\micro\metre} volume at $0.1 \times 0.1 \times 1$~\si{\micro\metre} resolving cells.

\paragraph{SROD.} For each grain the reference orientation sits near the origination point, ``the lowest location of a grain's boundary along the build direction'', offset one voxel layer (\SI{1.5}{\micro\metre}) upward so the reference is not a boundary pixel; offsets up to \SI{8}{\micro\metre} do not change the result. \emph{Originating} grains have their lowest point inside the volume; \emph{established} grains are cut by the dataset floor and referenced at the centroid of their footprint on the lowest slice. Grains shorter than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} (two layers) or under \SI{40}{\micro\metre} equivalent spherical diameter (ESD) are excluded, leaving over 500 originating and 100 established grains. Each grain is sliced along the build direction; each slice gives a SROD histogram fitted to a normal distribution, and the mean and the 90 \% interval of that fit are plotted against height above the origin (Fig.~5a) or above the dataset floor (Fig.~5c). Fig.~4 follows four grains (G1--G4) with IPF and SROD maps, the mean SROD per layer (``as large as 20--\SI{25}{\degree}'' after $> \SI{300}{\micro\metre}$), and every voxel on the build-direction inverse pole figure with an arrow for the rotation path from bottom to top; G4 splits into two orientation clusters. The maximum SROD in any grain is \SI{58.7}{\degree}; ``most columnar grains'' contain over \SI{25}{\degree}. The 2026 paper's Fig.~7a is a heatmap of SROD against distance from the grain start over 0--\SI{120}{\micro\metre} for the grains of that volume (the paper does not say whether grains cut by the volume floor are included), with quoted average and maximum accumulation rates of 1.7 and \SI{5.8}{\degree} per \SI{30}{\micro\metre} for bulk grains (2.5 and \SI{7.2}{\degree} for cells; the paper does not state how the average was formed); it also reports that cells switch \dir{100} growth variants at fusion lines rather than rotating continuously. Both papers attribute the misorientation to plastic deformation: solidification shrinkage in the mushy zone and thermomechanical cycling in the heat-affected zone of subsequent layers (2024), and, in the 2026 paper, torsion and deflection of cells by their neighbours during growth in the pool, with shear strains of 0.2--3 \% supported by synchrotron measurements; the last is an at-solidification mechanism.

\section{Analysis of the experimental data}
Fig.~5a and 5c of the 2024 paper were digitized from \SI{300}{dpi} renders (\code{docs/srod\_random\_walk.py}; curves in \code{docs/lamb2023\_fig5\{a,c\}\_srod.json}); no digitization uncertainty is claimed beyond the line width, about \SI{0.3}{\degree}. The curves were compared with a rotation walk: independent small rotations of angle $\theta$ about random axes give
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMS}^2 = n\,\theta^2 = D\,d,
\end{equation}
a Maxwell distribution ($\chi$ with three degrees of freedom) with mean $= 0.921\,\mathrm{RMS}$, and a 90 \% interval of $1.38 \times$ the mean at every distance, which is also the value of $3.29\sigma/\text{mean}$ for the normal fits Lamb plot. $D$ was fitted by least squares of $\mathrm{RMS}^2 = D h$ to the digitized mean over 0--\SI{265}{\micro\metre} with the 0.921 factor applied.

Findings (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp}): $D = \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ fits the originating mean, and the measured width ratio is 1.3--1.5 at every height for the originating grains. The established grains are described by the same $D$ only after adding an initial spread of \SI{6.2}{\degree} RMS in quadrature (one fitted parameter, the orientation scatter already present at the dataset floor); with it, their width ratio falls to 1.1--1.25 beyond \SI{150}{\micro\metre}, as expected for a non-central distribution, and their mean runs above the model past \SI{300}{\micro\metre}. Over 30--\SI{260}{\micro\metre} the square-root curve and Lamb's own reading (a fast first \SI{30}{\micro\metre}, then \SI{0.85}{\degree} per layer) lie within the line width of each other; the two differ only below \SI{30}{\micro\metre}, where the digitization is least reliable. The population statistics therefore establish independent finite-variance increments per unit height and their magnitude, not the mechanism, and not whether the increments belong to voxel lineages or to whole grains: each grain's coherent walk is one realisation, and the Maxwell width appears across grains either way. Fig.~4 supplies the missing information for four grains: the mean orientation moves along a path, so at least part of the accumulation is coherent across a grain.

\section{Simulation methods}
\paragraph{Code.} SPPARKS, branch SURGE-kokkos, app \code{additive/texture/kk} (Kokkos/HIP on an AMD gfx1151 APU), one MPI rank. The CPU app \code{additive/texture} carries the same model and is the correctness reference; the temperature field is deterministic and bit-identical between the two (\code{examples/am\_validation/}). Microstructure is not deterministic on the GPU, so every build is one realisation of its seed.

\paragraph{Thermal source.} Moser Green's-function solution with the GP700 constants (\code{setup\_temperature\_source moser 176 0.9 22.5 4.335e-6 473 600 20e-6 20e-6 10e-6}: power, absorptivity, conductivity, diffusivity, preheat, heat capacity, and the Gaussian source widths $\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z$ of 20, 20, \SI{10}{\micro\metre}), evaluated on the device for every site each step. Scan lines with \code{hatch 80e-6 N serpentine pause 8.8e-3}, the pause being the \SI{10}{\milli\metre} track length over the scan speed; the fast-forward predictor skips idle gaps. Single-hatch check: pool $147 \times 69$~\si{\micro\metre}, thermal gradient \SIrange{1e7}{2.9e7}{\kelvin\per\metre} at the fusion boundary (paper: \SI{3e7}{\kelvin\per\metre}).

\paragraph{Build.} Box-per-layer stitch continuation (\code{run\_layers.sh}): each layer is a separate SPPARKS run of a \SI{180}{\micro\metre} window below the laser plane, started from the preheat and from the previous layer's microstructure. Domain \SI{801}{\micro\metre} square (267 voxels at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}), 10 hatches at $40 + 80k$ \si{\micro\metre} with lines \SI{100}{\micro\metre} past both edges, 12 layers of \SI{30}{\micro\metre}, \SI{90}{\degree} rotation per layer; layers of the same direction have their hatches at the same positions (registered), which is the deck's assumption where the papers are silent. Substrate: \SI{150}{\micro\metre} of \SI{50}{\micro\metre} Voronoi grains with uniform random orientations (\code{make\_substrate.py}); five overlapping hatches remelt \SIrange{80}{100}{\micro\metre}, so the analysable solid begins at \SI{105}{\micro\metre} (the analysis floor is set at \SI{100}{\micro\metre}). Timestep \SI{4}{\micro\second}, twice the front-advance limit $\Delta x / V(T_l - T_s)$; 2 and \SI{4}{\micro\second} agreed on grain count and size within the run-to-run scatter. Calibration used a \SI{600}{\micro\metre}, 7-hatch, 8-layer proxy at \SI{3}{\minute} of loop time per box.

\paragraph{Solidification kinetics.} Front velocity is a cubic polynomial in undercooling (the 304L stainless-steel fit shipped with the additive app) scaled $\times 3.5$ (front at \SIrange{25}{35}{\kelvin} undercooling, about \SI{0.56}{\metre\per\second}, keeping up with the \SI{1.137}{\metre\per\second} scan); nucleation density \SI{1.5e13}{\per\cubic\metre}, critical undercooling $t_c = \SI{15}{\kelvin}$ with a \SI{2}{\kelvin} standard deviation across sites. Parent selection per grain: candidate neighbours are grouped by grain, each grain weighted by the mobility $1 + c_2 \cos(c_3 \theta)$ of its mean orientation (the normalised sum of the candidate voxels' quaternions after flipping each to the same hemisphere) against the local thermal gradient ($\theta$ the angle between the nearest \dir{100} of that mean orientation and the gradient; $c_2 = 2.8$, $c_3 = 2.5$, negative clamped to zero), then the parent voxel is drawn uniformly inside the chosen grain. Weighting among the voxels of one grain instead rotated grains toward \dir{001} with height and is not used.

\paragraph{Misorientation model.} Two terms, both applied at capture in the sample frame. The model is a phenomenological proxy: it places at solidification the rotation that Lamb attribute to shrinkage in the mushy zone and to cycling in the heat-affected zone of later layers. It therefore cannot represent any dependence of a voxel's SROD on the number of layers deposited above it, and a 12-layer build is compared here with material from the interior of a \SI{10}{\milli\metre} block.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Per-voxel walk}, \code{misorientation\_diffusion} $D_v$ \code{30}: the captured voxel takes its parent's orientation rotated by $\theta = \sqrt{D_v\,\mathrm{hop}}$ about a random axis (hop = lattice distance to the parent, so $D_v$ is per unit of growth path). A rotation past \SI{30}{\degree} would start a new grain (never reached). Independent lineages of one grain diverge, so this term supplies intragranular spread and no net drift.
\item \emph{Coherent grain walk}, \code{misorientation\_grain\_walk} $D_g$ $\rho$ \code{path}: every voxel carries its inheritance path length $s$ in lattice units (per-site field d10: zero at melt and at nuclei, parent plus hop at capture, copied with the orientation on a smoothing flip, exchanged with ghosts and continued through the stitch). Between path levels $k$ and $k+1$ every voxel of grain $g$ receives the same rotation, a Gaussian rotation vector $v_k$ of RMS $\sqrt{D_g \Delta x}$ whose sequence in $k$ is Ornstein--Uhlenbeck, $v_k = \rho\,v_{k-1} + \sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,v'_k$, with $v'_k$ hashed from $(g, k)$ so the sequence is a pure function of grain and level, path independent and without stored state. The correlation length is $\approx \Delta x/(1-\rho)$ of path and the long-range accumulation is $D_g (1+\rho)/(1-\rho)$ per unit path; $\rho$ is defined per lattice level, so a change of $\Delta x$ requires $\rho' = 1 - (1-\rho)\Delta x'/\Delta x$ to keep the correlation length and a corresponding rescaling of $D_g$ (Section~\ref{sec:res}). This term supplies the coherent drift. A device cache of the recurrence makes it cost nothing measurable.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Boundary smoothing.} One greedy steepest-descent pass per captured voxel (\code{smooth\_greedy\_multiproposal on}, nrefine 1) the step after capture, and \code{smooth\_orientation copy}: a voxel that changes grain takes one random neighbour's orientation and path length from that grain. The default \code{average} (sign-aligned mean of the new grain's neighbours) cancels the fresh rotations of a voxel-scale mixed front, retains only 0.3--0.45 of the input $D$ and widens the population band to 1.8; it is not used.

\section{Simulation analysis methods}
\code{gp700\_analysis.py} on the spparks-micro-analysis tools: read the last stitch frame; solid means \code{i2 == 3} (solidified; unremelted substrate, \code{i2 == 1}, is excluded); crop to the central \SI{400}{\micro\metre} laterally (\SI{300}{\micro\metre} on the proxy), grains cut by the crop faces kept with their cropped volume; label grains as connected components of the spin field, or optionally re-segment at a disorientation threshold (\code{--segment-deg}); grain volume, equivalent spherical diameter (ESD, from the cropped volume) and extents; Lamb's filters (height $\geq \SI{60}{\micro\metre}$, ESD $\geq \SI{40}{\micro\metre}$); origin = the grain's lowest solid voxel, whose quaternion is the reference (exact in the simulation, so no offset); grains crossing the analysis floor are established and referenced at their floor-slice centroid. SROD is the symmetry-reduced disorientation of every voxel from its grain's reference, binned by height above the origin or floor in \SI{10}{\micro\metre} steps with mean, $p_5$, $p_{95}$ and RMS per bin. Score: RMS of (simulated mean $-$ Lamb Fig.~5a mean) over \SIrange{25}{240}{\micro\metre}, its bias, the band ratio $(p_{95}-p_5)/\text{mean}$ averaged over the same bins, the fraction of solid within \SI{15}{\degree} of \dir{001} parallel to the build direction (random: 0.102 by Monte Carlo), and grain counts. ESD values quoted for a build are volume-weighted medians over all grains of the crop (the ESD of the grain holding the median voxel); the number-weighted median ESD of the filtered grains is \SI{75}{\micro\metre} in v10 and \SI{72}{\micro\metre} in v8, and the filtered grains hold 96 \% of the crop volume. Calibration: Sobol designs (scipy \code{qmc}; 16 + 8 points over $D_v$ 0.2--2.0, nucleation \numrange{1e13}{1e14}, $t_c$ 3--\SI{25}{\kelvin}, $c_2$ 1--4, then 8 points over $c_2$ 2--8 and nucleation \numrange{5e12}{5e13}) on the proxy against the Fig.~5a score fixed $D_v$ for the per-voxel model; for the two-term model $\rho$ was set from a target correlation length of \SI{30}{\micro\metre} of path (the height of Lamb's initial fast regime), $D_g$ from the requirement that the coherent term supply most of the long-range accumulation with the per-voxel term retained for intragranular spread ($D_g (1+\rho)/(1-\rho) : D_v \approx 3$), and the sum rescaled once to the Fig.~5a level; the Fig.~5c established curve and the 2026 Fig.~7a heatmap were not consulted.

\code{grain\_paths.py} reproduces Lamb's Fig.~4 per grain and extends it to every filtered grain: IPF-Z and SROD maps on the $xz$ slice through the grain, mean SROD per \SI{10}{\micro\metre} slab, the per-slab mean orientation as a path on the build-direction inverse pole figure, the drift of the mean orientation from the bottom three slabs to the top three, the RMS spread of the top slabs about their mean, the maximum SROD, the per-layer mean at \SI{300}{\micro\metre}, the accumulation rate (drift per \SI{30}{\micro\metre} of height), the path length per unit height from d10, and the fraction of slabs in which the mean SROD rises. Both scripts use the same labelling, so the filtered-grain counts agree except that \code{grain\_paths.py} drops grains with fewer than 20 voxels in their bottom or top slabs (97 vs 96 in v10, 88 vs 86 in v8). \code{hatch\_periodicity.py} measures the boundary-voxel density across the crop, its autocorrelation at the hatch lag, and in-plane grain widths by line intercept; \code{origin\_census.py} counts filtered grains that originate more than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} above the first remelt floor.

The established class is the discriminating test. The per-voxel model's floor-referenced grains start \SI{3}{\degree} too high because their lineages have already diverged across the grain by the time they cross the floor, whereas the coherent model, whose intragranular spread is small, reproduces the curve to \SI{0.28}{\degree} in the first build and \SI{0.47}{\degree} (bias \SI{+0.25}{\degree}) in the second, with the Maxwell band. The result depends on where the floor is placed, because the model's intragranular spread grows with path from the origin: floors at 200, 240 and \SI{300}{\micro\metre} give biases of $-0.65$, $-0.11$ and \SI{+0.44}{\degree}, so the agreement is at the \SI{0.5}{\degree} level rather than the \SI{0.1}{\degree} the best floor suggests. The 2026 heatmap is a weaker test. Its density-weighted mean (about \SI{5}{\degree} from the grain start) carries no stated digitization uncertainty and is flat because the volume contains grains that start at or below its floor, as the established class does in 2024; only the mode is used here. Against the mode, v10 runs 30--50 \% low over the first \SI{60}{\micro\metre} (0.8 vs \SI{1.6}{\degree} at \SI{5}{\micro\metre}) and meets it by \SI{115}{\micro\metre}, while v8 tracks it within \SI{0.2}{\degree} to \SI{60}{\micro\metre} and overshoots by \SI{115}{\micro\metre}; the RMS of the modal residuals is \SI{0.8}{\degree} for v10 and \SI{0.6}{\degree} for v8. The 2026 mode at \SI{5}{\micro\metre} (\SI{1.6}{\degree}) is the sub-\SI{30}{\micro\metre} information the 2024 curves lack; it sits above the pure $D = 0.42$ walk (\SI{1.2}{\degree}) and above v10, i.e. the experiment accumulates faster than either over the first two voxel layers of the coarse dataset.

The coherent term changes the per-grain picture in the way Lamb's Fig.~4 requires: the mean orientation of a v10 grain drifts more than it spreads, the drift scales with height (about \SI{2}{\degree} at \SI{100}{\micro\metre}, 8--\SI{15}{\degree} at \SI{400}{\micro\metre}), and the paths are directed; v8's grains spread more than they drift at every height. Against Lamb's numbers, however, both models fall short. The accumulation rate of the v10 grains (drift of the mean orientation per \SI{30}{\micro\metre} of height, averaged over grains) is half the 2026 average and its maximum is a third of theirs, if the paper's average is a comparable per-grain quantity; the per-layer means at \SI{300}{\micro\metre} reach \SI{18}{\degree} at most against ``as large as'' 20--\SI{25}{\degree} for Lamb's four example grains, which were chosen as illustrations and are not a population statistic; and 5 \% of the v10 grains exceed \SI{25}{\degree} anywhere, against ``most'' in the experiment (a maximum-SROD statistic that in EBSD is sensitive to boundary and misindexed voxels, so the experimental side is an upper estimate). Lamb's own figures are not mutually consistent (a mean above \SI{25}{\degree} over \SI{300}{\micro\metre} in the text against \SI{11}{\degree} at \SI{260}{\micro\metre} in Fig.~5a); the population curves are taken as the target and the per-grain numbers as indicative. The population mean is matched, so the experimental population must carry a much heavier tail, a minority of grains rotating several times faster than the median, than a single Gaussian $D_g$ produces; the 10--\SI{15}{\degree} secondary band of the 2026 heatmap and the split grain G4 point the same way. The paths have no preferred direction on the triangle, as the isotropic rotation vector implies; the experimental direction is not reported. Monotonicity does not separate the models (0.79 vs 0.78): about a fifth of the grains reverse over \SI{100}{\micro\metre} scales in both. The per-grain drift-to-spread ratio confirms the construction ($\sqrt{D_g(1+\rho)/(1-\rho)/D_v} = 1.7$) rather than an experimental value; Lamb's within-slice widths, which would fix it, are not published.

\subsection{Grain structure and the hatch pattern}
\label{sec:hatch}
The grain boundaries of both full builds fall at \SI{80}{\micro\metre} intervals in $x$ and in $y$: the boundary-density autocorrelation at the \SI{80}{\micro\metre} lag is 0.61 / 0.70 ($x$ / $y$) in v10 and 0.58 / 0.71 in v8, and 43--62 \% of the spectral power of the boundary density sits at the hatch period (\code{hatch\_periodicity.py}, Fig.~\ref{fig:hatch}). In-plane grain widths by line intercept have medians of 30, 39 and \SI{51}{\micro\metre} at 200, 300 and \SI{405}{\micro\metre} height ($p_{90}$ 72--\SI{84}{\micro\metre}). The cause is the deck's registered hatch pattern: layers $n$ and $n+2$ have identical scan lines, so the pool centrelines, where the last liquid solidifies, recur at the same positions and select the same columns, and the grains become $\sim 80 \times 80$~\si{\micro\metre} columns spanning the build, which also compresses the ESD distribution ($p_{10}$--$p_{90}$ 76--\SI{170}{\micro\metre}). Lamb describe serrated, irregular boundaries. The papers do not state the hatch registration of the SLM125 build. As a sensitivity test the driver gained a per-layer hatch offset (\code{HATCH\_OFFSET=random}: a seeded uniform shift in $[-40, 40)$~\si{\micro\metre} along the hatch normal, so layers $n$ and $n+2$ no longer register), and the v10 settings were run on the proxy with it against a registered proxy of the same seed. The offset lowers the boundary autocorrelation at the hatch lag from 0.46 / 0.45 to 0.34 / 0.48 ($x$ / $y$) and the spectral power at the period from 0.47 / 0.57 to 0.52 / 0.39; grain widths (median 27--36 vs 30--33~\si{\micro\metre}), ESD ($p_{50}$ 120 vs \SI{123}{\micro\metre}), the SROD score (\SI{0.30}{\degree} / \SI{-0.14}{\degree}, band 1.50) and the per-grain drift and spread (5.3 / \SI{3.6}{\degree} vs 6.0 / \SI{3.8}{\degree}) are unchanged within the seed scatter, while the \dir{001} fraction rises from 0.21--0.25 to 0.30 (one realisation). A normalised autocorrelation of the height-integrated boundary density is insensitive to phase, so it cannot separate registration from the pool spacing itself, which imprints an \SI{80}{\micro\metre} period on every layer; the unchanged grain widths and ESD under the offset are the evidence that the column selection is not registration-driven, and the SROD results do not depend on it. Whether the experimental grains carry the same imprint cannot be read from the published sections.

\subsection{Grain definition}
Re-segmenting the simulated volume at Lamb's \SI{2}{\degree} disorientation threshold (\code{--segment-deg 2}) changes the grain population. v10: 411\,000 labels, 139 pass Lamb's filters (spin labels: 96), ESD $p_{50}$ \SI{29}{\micro\metre}, two grains span \SI{300}{\micro\metre}; the Fig.~5a score stays within \SI{0.6}{\degree} (bias \SI{-0.45}{\degree}, band 1.49), and the per-grain drift / spread become 5.5 / \SI{1.9}{\degree}. v8: 758\,000 labels, 54 filtered grains of ESD $p_{50}$ \SI{8}{\micro\metre}, no grain taller than \SI{165}{\micro\metre}, score \SI{1.19}{\degree}. The per-voxel walk alone does not survive a \SI{2}{\degree} segmentation as columnar grains; the coherent model keeps its SROD curve but not its grain size, because the simulated same-grain neighbour disorientation (median \SI{2.4}{\degree} in v10) sits at the threshold. The simulated field carries voxel-scale orientation noise that the EBSD does not (the \SI{2.4}{\degree} neighbour disorientation is a property of independent-lineage inheritance at this $D_v$ and would be lower only with a smaller per-voxel term), so the two segmentations are not equivalent; the spin labelling is the one used elsewhere in this report. A threshold sweep locates the equivalence (Table~\ref{tab:seg}): at \SI{3}{\degree} the coherent model recovers its columnar population (97 filtered grains, 37 taller than \SI{300}{\micro\metre}, ESD $p_{50}$ \SI{128}{\micro\metre}) with a Fig.~5a score of \SI{0.25}{\degree}, whereas the per-voxel model needs \SI{5}{\degree}; since the EBSD orientation noise is a few tenths of a degree against a median same-grain neighbour disorientation of \SI{2.4}{\degree} (v10) and \SI{3.8}{\degree} (v8) in the simulation, a \SI{3}{\degree} threshold on v10 plays the role of Lamb's \SI{2}{\degree}. The \SI{2}{\degree} segmentation does, however, change the grain-class picture of the next section: on it, 55 of the 141 filtered v10 grains (13 \% of the filtered volume) originate more than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} above the remelt floor, because the coherent walk builds low-angle boundaries inside columnar grains that a \SI{2}{\degree} threshold reads as new grains, as Lamb's G4 does; v8 gives none.

\subsection{Grain origins}
With the spin labelling, 3 of the 100 filtered v10 grains and 3 of the 94 filtered v8 grains originate more than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} above the remelt floor (\SI{0.5}{\percent} of the filtered volume): the columnar grains are epitaxial continuations of substrate grains, so the simulated ``originating'' SROD is an established-grain SROD referenced at the remelt floor. A census over 26 existing proxy runs spanning nucleation densities of \numrange{6.5e12}{8.5e13} \si{\per\cubic\metre} and critical undercoolings of 3--\SI{23}{\kelvin} finds 0--7 filtered grains per run originating more than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} above the floor (0--4 \% of the filtered volume); Lamb's volume has over 500 such grains against 100 established. No region of the tested parameter space produces surviving mid-build origins by nucleation; nucleated grains are overgrown. Lamb's originating grains are products of a \SI{2}{\degree} segmentation, and on the same segmentation the coherent model does produce mid-build origins (previous section), so the like-for-like comparison is 55 : 86 (v10, \SI{2}{\degree}) against Lamb's $> 500 : 100$; the model is short by a factor of about three in the ratio, not absent. Analysing v8 on the full \SI{801}{\micro\metre} domain, the central \SI{400}{\micro\metre}, the central \SI{200}{\micro\metre}, or without the never-remelted top layer changes the SROD bias by under \SI{0.2}{\degree} and the band by under 0.03.

\subsection{Resolution}
\label{sec:res}
The per-voxel model (v8 deck) was run at Lamb's \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} voxel on the proxy with the same $D_v$ and geometry: SROD RMS / bias \SI{0.49}{\degree} / \SI{+0.41}{\degree} against \SI{0.44}{\degree} / \SI{+0.03}{\degree} at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}, band 1.43 vs 1.41, same grain size and texture, with the finer lattice accumulating 10--18 \% more per unit height because it resolves a slightly longer growth path. For the coherent term, $\rho$ is per lattice level: keeping the \SI{30}{\micro\metre} correlation length at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} takes $\rho' = 0.95$ and, to keep the long-range accumulation, $D_g' = D_g (1+\rho)(1-\rho')/((1-\rho)(1+\rho')) = \SI{0.0049}{\Dunit}$. With that rescaling the v10 deck at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} on the proxy gives Fig.~5a RMS / bias \SI{0.74}{\degree} / \SI{+0.64}{\degree} (\SI{3}{\micro\metre}, three seeds: $0.35 \pm 0.05$ / $+0.05 \pm 0.11$\si{\degree}), band 1.42 (1.53), per-grain drift / spread 7.8 / \SI{4.4}{\degree} (6.6 / \SI{3.9}{\degree}), maximum SROD median \SI{15.6}{\degree} (13.7), accumulation rate mean \SI{1.00}{\degree} per \SI{30}{\micro\metre} (0.84), ESD $p_{50}$ \SI{115}{\micro\metre} (123), and a measured path length per unit height of 1.68 (1.47--1.49). The mean SROD runs above the \SI{3}{\micro\metre} value by an amount that grows with height: 3 \% at \SI{45}{\micro\metre} (3.4 vs \SI{3.3}{\degree}), 10 \% at \SI{205}{\micro\metre} (9.6 vs 8.7) and 23 \% at \SI{282}{\micro\metre} (12.4 vs 10.1). To test whether the longer resolved growth path (1.68 vs 1.47) is the cause, the run was repeated with $D_v$ and $D_g$ additionally scaled by $1.47/1.68 = 0.875$: the bias falls from \SI{+0.64}{\degree} to \SI{+0.18}{\degree} (RMS \SI{0.30}{\degree}, band 1.49, drift / spread 7.1 / \SI{4.4}{\degree}, path factor 1.76), within two standard deviations of the \SI{3}{\micro\metre} seed scatter, and the excess over the \SI{3}{\micro\metre} curve shrinks to $-4$ \% at \SI{45}{\micro\metre}, $+7$ \% at \SI{205}{\micro\metre} and $+12$ \% at \SI{282}{\micro\metre}. The path factor therefore accounts for most of the resolution dependence, with a residual of order 10 \% at the top of the build that grows with height and is not explained. The two-term model is resolution independent to first order once $\rho$, $D_g$ and the per-path constants are rescaled with the voxel size and the measured path factor.

\subsection{Texture}
Neither paper quantifies a \dir{001} fibre; the 2026 paper discusses both \dir{001}- and \dir{111}-oriented grains. A Sobol study over the mobility contrast $c_2$ and nucleation density with $D$ fixed gave the \dir{001}-within-\SI{15}{\degree} fraction flat at 0.22 below $c_2 \approx 5$ and rising by 0.0125 per unit $c_2$ above it, with the SROD score unaffected; on the full build $c_2 = 9$ gave 0.22 against 0.19 at $c_2 = 2.8$. The coherent walk lowers the fraction (v10: 0.15) because it carries grains out of the cone. Against a random baseline of 0.102, the builds sit at 1.5--2.2$\times$ random.

\section{Findings and open issues}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Population SROD in GP700 has the statistics of a finite-variance rotation walk, $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ against height with a Maxwell width; individual grains rotate along a path. The statistics do not identify the mechanism, and the published curves do not distinguish $\sqrt{h}$ from the authors' two-regime reading above \SI{30}{\micro\metre}; the 2026 mode of \SI{1.6}{\degree} at \SI{5}{\micro\metre} indicates faster accumulation below \SI{30}{\micro\metre} than the single-$D$ walk gives.
\item The two-term model reproduces the calibration curve and, without further tuning, the established-grain curve to within \SI{0.5}{\degree} (floor-placement dependent), which the per-voxel model alone fails by \SI{3}{\degree}; against the 2026 modal trajectory the two models are comparable. Per grain the model produces directed paths but accumulates at half Lamb's average rate with far smaller extremes: the experiment has a heavy tail of fast-rotating grains that a single Gaussian $D_g$ does not give.
\item Deck constants are per unit of growth path; the measured path length is 1.47 times the height. Both terms are resolution independent to first order once $\rho$ and $D_g$ are rescaled with $\Delta x$, with the measured path factor (1.47 at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}, 1.68 at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre}) carrying most of the residual resolution dependence.
\item The simulated grain structure is locked to the deck's registered hatch pattern; the experimental hatch registration is unknown.
\item Grain classes: with spin labels no surviving grains originate mid-build at any tested nucleation density; with Lamb's \SI{2}{\degree} segmentation the coherent model gives an originating-to-established ratio of 55 : 86, against Lamb's $> 500 : 100$, because its intragranular boundaries read as new grains.
\item A \SI{2}{\degree} re-segmentation of the simulation changes its grain population; the two grain definitions are not equivalent, and the per-voxel term sets the intragranular neighbour disorientation that decides it.
\item $D_g : D_v$ and $\rho$ are fixed here by construction and by the held-out curves only loosely; Lamb's within-slice widths, per-grain rate distribution and path directions would fix them.
\item Not yet tested: the undercooling dependence of $D_v$; a heavy-tailed or grain-dependent $D_g$.
\end{enumerate}
