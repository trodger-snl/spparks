\begin{abstract}
Lamb et al.\ measured the starting reference orientation deviation (SROD) of columnar grains in a 3D-EBSD volume of laser powder bed fusion GammaPrint-700. Re-analysing their Fig.~5 shows that SROD is a random-axis rotation walk: $\mathrm{RMS}^2 = D h$ with $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ against build height, the same $D$ for grains originating inside the volume and for established grains, and a 5/95 band of $(p_{95}-p_5)/\text{mean} \approx 1.4$, the Maxwell width of a three-dimensional walk. SPPARKS \code{additive/texture} and \code{additive/texture/kk} now implement that model (\code{misorientation\_diffusion}), and a Moser-source GP700 deck reproduces the experiment: RMS deviation from Lamb's mean \SI{0.49}{\degree} over \SIrange{25}{240}{\micro\metre}, bias \SI{+0.17}{\degree}, band/mean 1.41. Two model corrections were required (per-grain parent selection and copy-mode boundary smoothing). The deck $D$ is \SI{0.22}{\Dunit}, half Lamb's height fit, because the inheritance path is $1.8\times$ the height; the same $D$ reproduces Lamb at the experiment's own \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} voxel. Open issues: the simulated grains are epitaxial substrate continuations, so Lamb's originating class is essentially absent, and the \dir{001} fibre has no quantitative target.
\end{abstract}

\section{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
\item SROD in GP700 is a random-axis rotation walk with $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ against height and a Maxwell width. The authors' ``fast first \SI{30}{\micro\metre}, then constant'' reading is $\sqrt{h}$.
\item Both SPPARKS additive apps carry the model: each captured voxel takes its parent's orientation rotated by $\theta = \sqrt{D\,\mathrm{hop}}$ about a random axis. The misorientation cap and gradient-target rotation are removed.
\item The GP700 Moser deck (\code{examples/gp700\_moser/}) matches Lamb's SROD level, $\sqrt{h}$ shape and band width over the full \SI{400}{\micro\metre} build (Table~\ref{tab:v8}). Two corrections were needed: per-grain parent selection (mobility weighting between grains, not between the voxels of one grain, which had rotated grains toward \dir{001}) and \code{smooth\_orientation copy} (the smoothing pass averaged neighbour quaternions, cancelling \SI{60}{\percent} of the walk and widening the band to 1.8).
\item Deck $D = \SI{0.22}{\Dunit}$ at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}; the growth path is $1.8\times$ the height. At \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} the same $D$ stays on Lamb (bias \SI{+0.41}{\degree}) with \SIrange{10}{18}{\percent} more accumulation per height (Table~\ref{tab:dx}).
\item Open: 83 of 88 filtered grains originate at the first remelt floor, so Lamb's originating class ($>500$ grains, 5:1 over established) is essentially absent; the \dir{001} fibre (0.19--0.24 within \SI{15}{\degree} of the build direction, twice random) has no quantitative experimental target.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental methods (Lamb et al.\ 2023, Met.\ Trans.\ A 2026)}
\paragraph{Material and process.} CoNi-base superalloy GammaPrint-700, laser powder bed fusion at \SI{176}{\watt} and \SI{1137}{\milli\metre\per\second}, \SI{80}{\micro\metre} spot ($D4\sigma$), \SI{80}{\micro\metre} hatch, \SI{30}{\micro\metre} layers, \SI{90}{\degree} interlayer rotation, \SI{200}{\celsius} plate preheat. The 2026 paper supplies the thermophysical set used for its 3DThesis simulations: $k = \SI{22.5}{\watt\per\metre\per\kelvin}$, $\rho = \SI{8650}{\kilogram\per\cubic\metre}$, $c_p = \SI{600}{\joule\per\kilogram\per\kelvin}$ ($\alpha = \SI{4.335e-6}{\metre\squared\per\second}$), absorptivity 0.9, liquidus \SI{1646}{\kelvin}, solidus \SI{1592}{\kelvin}.

\paragraph{3D dataset.} TriBeam tomography (femtosecond-laser serial sectioning with EBSD on each slice), \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} in-plane step and \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} slice thickness, 385 slices, reconstructed into one HDF5 volume and segmented into grains at a \SI{2}{\degree} misorientation threshold before any SROD calculation.

\paragraph{SROD.} For each grain the reference orientation sits near the origination point, ``the lowest location of a grain's boundary along the build direction'', offset one voxel layer (\SI{1.5}{\micro\metre}) upward so the reference is not a boundary pixel with a convoluted EBSD pattern; offsets up to \SI{8}{\micro\metre} do not change the result. Two classes: \emph{originating} grains, whose lowest point lies inside the volume, and \emph{established} grains, cut by the dataset floor and referenced at the centroid of their footprint on the lowest slice. Grains shorter than \SI{60}{\micro\metre} (two layers) or under \SI{40}{\micro\metre} equivalent spherical diameter (ESD) are excluded as overtaken during competitive growth, leaving over 500 originating and over 100 established grains. Each grain is sliced along the build direction; each slice gives a SROD histogram fitted to a normal distribution and plotted against height above the origin (Fig.~5a) or above the dataset floor (Fig.~5c). The 2026 paper adds the cellular-scale finding used here: cells switch \dir{100} growth variants at fusion lines rather than rotating continuously, so no gradient-directed rotation is modelled.

\section{Analysis of the experimental data}
Fig.~5a and 5c were digitized from \SI{300}{dpi} renders of the paper (\code{docs/srod\_random\_walk.py}; curves in \code{docs/lamb2023\_fig5\{a,c\}\_srod.json}). The mean, 5th and 95th percentile curves were tested against a rotation walk: each increment is a small rotation of angle $\theta$ about a random axis; after $n$ independent increments the misorientation from the origin has
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMS}^2 = n\,\theta^2 = D\,d
\end{equation}
and a Maxwell distribution ($\chi$ with three degrees of freedom), for which $(p_{95}-p_5)/\text{mean} = 1.38$ at every distance. $D = \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ fitted on the originating grains predicts the established grains without refitting; the measured width ratio is 1.3--1.5 at every height; the apparent two-regime shape is the square root (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp}). A constant per-hop angle is not required, only independent increments with finite variance, so $D$ can carry an undercooling dependence without changing the $\sqrt{h}$ law.

\section{Simulation methods}
\paragraph{Code.} SPPARKS, branch SURGE-kokkos, app \code{additive/texture/kk} (Kokkos/HIP on an AMD gfx1151 APU), one MPI rank. The CPU app \code{additive/texture} carries the same model and is the correctness reference; the temperature field is deterministic and bit-identical between the two (\code{examples/am\_validation/}).

\paragraph{Thermal source.} Moser Green's-function solution with the GP700 constants (\code{setup\_temperature\_source moser 176 0.9 22.5 4.335e-6 473 600 20e-6 20e-6 10e-6}), evaluated on the device for every site each step. Scan lines with \code{hatch 80e-6 N serpentine pause 8.8e-3}, the pause being the real \SI{10}{\milli\metre} track length over the scan speed; the fast-forward predictor skips idle gaps. Single-hatch check: pool $147 \times 69$~\si{\micro\metre}, thermal gradient \SIrange{1e7}{2.9e7}{\kelvin\per\metre} at the fusion boundary (paper: \SI{3e7}{\kelvin\per\metre}).

\paragraph{Build.} Box-per-layer stitch continuation (\code{run\_layers.sh}). Domain \SI{801}{\micro\metre} square (267 voxels at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}), 10 hatches at $40 + 80k$ \si{\micro\metre} with lines \SI{100}{\micro\metre} past both edges, 12 layers of \SI{30}{\micro\metre}, \SI{90}{\degree} rotation per layer, each box a \SI{180}{\micro\metre} window below the laser plane; each layer starts from the preheat (the recoat interval). Substrate: \SI{150}{\micro\metre} of \SI{50}{\micro\metre} Voronoi grains with uniform random orientations (\code{make\_substrate.py}); five overlapping hatches remelt \SIrange{80}{100}{\micro\metre}, so the analysable volume begins at \SI{105}{\micro\metre}. Timestep \SI{4}{\micro\second}, twice the $\Delta x / V(T_l - T_s)$ guard; 2 and \SI{4}{\micro\second} agree within seed noise. The \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} run uses 400 voxels over \SI{600}{\micro\metre}, 7 hatches, 8 layers, $\Delta t = \SI{2}{\micro\second}$.

\paragraph{Solidification kinetics.} Front velocity is the 304L polynomial $\times 3.5$ (front at \SIrange{25}{35}{\kelvin} undercooling, about \SI{0.56}{\metre\per\second}, keeping up with the \SI{1.137}{\metre\per\second} scan); nucleation density \SI{1.5e13}{\per\cubic\metre}, critical undercooling \SI{15}{\kelvin}, deviation \SI{2}{\kelvin}. Parent selection per grain: candidate neighbours are grouped by grain, each grain weighted by the mobility $1 + c_2 \cos(c_3 \theta)$ of its sign-aligned mean orientation against the local thermal gradient ($c_2 = 2.8$, $c_3 = 2.5$, negative clamped to zero), then the parent voxel is drawn uniformly inside the chosen grain.

\paragraph{Misorientation.} \code{misorientation\_diffusion 2.2e5 30}: every captured voxel takes its parent's orientation rotated by $\theta = \sqrt{D\,\mathrm{hop}}$ about a random axis (hop = lattice distance to the parent in \si{\metre}, $D$ in \si{deg\squared\per\metre}); nucleus shells rotate relative to the seed by the same rule; a rotation past \SI{30}{\degree} would start a new grain (never reached). No cap, no gradient target, constant $D$.

\paragraph{Boundary smoothing.} One greedy steepest-descent pass per captured voxel (\code{smooth\_greedy\_multiproposal on}, nrefine 1) the step after capture, and \code{smooth\_orientation copy}: a voxel that changes grain takes one random neighbour's orientation from that grain. The default \code{average} takes the sign-aligned mean.

\paragraph{Kokkos kernel corrections} made during this work, all now at CPU parity: race-free multicolour neighbour reads (they were $x$-index ordered), front advance only beside solid, nrefine passes honoured, zero-gradient mobility, and the \code{laser\_path} time anchor after \code{reset\_time}.

\section{Simulation analysis methods}
\code{gp700\_analysis.py} on the spparks-micro-analysis tools: read the last stitch frame; solid means \code{i2 == 3}, so unremelted substrate is excluded; crop to the central \SI{400}{\micro\metre} laterally (\SI{300}{\micro\metre} on the proxy), optional ceiling; relabel grains as connected components of spin; grain volume, ESD and extents; Lamb's filters (height $\geq \SI{60}{\micro\metre}$, ESD $\geq \SI{40}{\micro\metre}$); origin = the grain's lowest solid voxel, whose quaternion is the reference (exact in the simulation, so no offset); grains crossing the analysis floor are established and referenced at their floor-slice centroid. SROD is the symmetry-reduced disorientation of every voxel from its grain's reference, binned by height above the origin or floor in \SI{10}{\micro\metre} steps and, separately, by straight-line distance, with mean, $p_5$, $p_{95}$ and RMS per bin. $D$-fit: $\mathrm{RMS}^2 = D h$ through the origin, volume weighted. Score: RMS of (simulated mean $-$ Lamb Fig.~5a mean) over \SIrange{25}{240}{\micro\metre}, plus bias, $(p_{95}-p_5)/\text{mean}$, the fraction of solid within \SI{15}{\degree} of \dir{001} parallel to the build direction, and grain counts. Sobol designs (scipy \code{qmc}) on a \SI{600}{\micro\metre}, 8-layer proxy at \SI{6}{\minute} per box were used for calibration.

The per-hop rule $\theta = \sqrt{D\,\mathrm{hop}}$ is resolution independent to first order; the finer lattice resolves a slightly longer growth path per unit height, and an exact match at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre} would take $D \approx 0.19$.

\subsection{Crop sensitivity and grain origins}
Analysing v8 on the full \SI{801}{\micro\metre} domain, the central \SI{400}{\micro\metre}, the central \SI{200}{\micro\metre}, or with the never-remelted top layer removed changes the SROD bias by under \SI{0.2}{\degree} and the band by under 0.03; only the fibre fraction moves, and it cannot be measured on 24 grains. Of the 88 filtered grains in the standard crop, 83 originate at the layer-0 remelt floor (\SIrange{105}{130}{\micro\metre}, \SI{99}{\percent} of the filtered volume) and 3 above \SI{160}{\micro\metre}. The columnar grains are epitaxial continuations of substrate grains, so the simulated ``originating'' SROD is an established-grain SROD referenced at the remelt floor. Because Lamb's two classes share the same $D$, the walk comparison is unaffected, but the model does not generate Lamb's population of grains that originate mid-build.

\subsection{Texture}
A Sobol study over $c_2 \in [2,8]$ and nucleation \numrange{5e12}{5e13} with $D$ fixed gives $\langle 001\rangle = -0.042 + 0.0125\,c_2 + 0.0073 \ln(\text{nucl})$ ($R^2 = 0.72$), flat at 0.22 below $c_2 \approx 5$, with the SROD score unaffected throughout. On the full build $c_2 = 9$ gives 0.22, rising from 0.16 to 0.24 with height, while $c_2 = 2.8$ saturates at 0.19. The walk spreads orientations out of the \SI{15}{\degree} cone (\SI{13}{\degree} RMS over \SI{400}{\micro\metre}) about as fast as selection fills it. Neither paper quantifies the fibre.

\subsection{Per-grain orientation paths and the grain-level walk}
Lamb's Fig.~4 follows individual grains: an IPF map, a SROD map, the mean SROD per layer, and every voxel of the grain on the build-direction inverse pole figure, where a red arrow marks the rotation path from bottom to top and one grain (G4) splits into two clusters. \code{grain\_paths.py} reproduces that layout. For v8's four tallest grains the mean orientation drifts only 3--6$^\circ$ from bottom to top while the voxels spread 12$^\circ$ (RMS) about the top-slab mean: the per-voxel walk lets the lineages of one grain diverge independently, so the cloud expands isotropically and its mean stays put, whereas Lamb's grains rotate coherently along a path.

\code{misorientation\_grain\_walk} $D_g$ adds a coherent walk: every voxel of grain $g$ crossing lattice level $k \to k+1$ receives the same rotation, of angle $\sqrt{D_g\,\Delta x}$ about an axis hashed from $(g, k)$, so the grain's coherent rotation is a function of $(g, z)$ only, path independent and without per-grain state; the per-voxel walk adds intragranular spread on top. Table~\ref{tab:gw} compares three proxy runs with the v8 deck otherwise (Fig.~\ref{fig:gw}).

All three runs sit on Lamb's population curve with the same band ratio, so the population SROD statistics cannot distinguish coherent from per-voxel accumulation: each grain's coherent walk is one realisation and the Maxwell width appears across grains either way. The per-grain analysis does distinguish them, and the ratio $D_g : D_v$ sets drift against spread. Two further observations from the grain-only run: a memoryless walk makes individual SROD($h$) curves non-monotonic (one grain reaches \SI{8}{\degree} at \SI{150}{\micro\metre} and returns to \SI{2}{\degree} at \SI{240}{\micro\metre}), whereas Lamb's G1--G4 rise steadily to 20--\SI{25}{\degree}, which points to persistent, correlated-axis rotation (lattice bending) rather than a memoryless walk; and the per-level increment (\SI{1.1}{\degree} at \SI{3}{\micro\metre}) shows as horizontal bands in the IPF map. Lamb's within-slice widths are not published numerically, so the split is constrained only to drift $\gtrsim$ spread; the mixed setting is the working choice.

\section{Findings and open issues}
\begin{enumerate}
\item SROD in GP700 is a rotation walk with $D \approx \SI{0.42}{\Dunit}$ against height and a Maxwell width; both grain classes obey it.
\item The model reproduces level, shape and width once orientation is inherited from one parent voxel and never averaged. Per-grain parent selection and copy-mode smoothing are both required; averaging in either place damps the walk and narrows the band.
\item Deck $D$ is \SI{0.22}{\Dunit} at \SI{3}{\micro\metre} and about 0.19 at \SI{1.5}{\micro\metre}: the walk runs along the inheritance path, $1.8\times$ the height in this remelt geometry. Whether the real path factor is that large is an open physical question; $D$ should be read per unit path length.
\item Grain classes do not match. No surviving grains originate mid-build at nucleation \SI{1.5e13}{\per\cubic\metre} with Lamb's filters. Lamb's 5:1 originating-to-established ratio implies nucleation or lateral overgrowth the model does not produce at these settings.
\item Individual grains in the experiment rotate coherently along a path; the per-voxel walk alone spreads without drifting. The grain-level walk (\code{misorientation\_grain\_walk}) reproduces the drift with the same population statistics, and the steady rise of Lamb's individual grains suggests persistent rather than memoryless rotation.\n\item The \dir{001} fibre is twice random and moves only weakly with the mobility contrast; there is no measured target.
\item Not yet tested: the undercooling dependence of $D$ ($\alpha > 0$); the single-hatch check deck is hot enough that $\alpha$ barely acts.
\end{enumerate}
