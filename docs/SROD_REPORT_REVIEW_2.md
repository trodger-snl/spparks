# Second review of "Misorientation accumulation along columnar grains in GammaPrint-700 laser powder bed fusion: 3D-EBSD (Lamb et al.) versus SPPARKS" (Revision 2, 2026-09-09)

Reviewer basis: the revised LaTeX source `docs/SROD_ANALYSIS_REPORT.tex`, its seven figures, the first review (`SROD_REPORT_REVIEW.md`), the authors' response (`SROD_REPORT_RESPONSE.md`), and the two Lamb et al. papers (Scripta Materialia 239 (2024) 115770, manuscript text; Met. Trans. A 57A (2026) 2430). No code, run directories, plan documents or logs were consulted.

---

## 1. Summary of the revised work

The report re-analyses the population SROD curves of Lamb et al.'s 3D-EBSD volume and argues that they carry the statistics of a finite-variance random rotation walk (`RMS^2 = D h`, `D ~ 0.42 deg^2/um`, Maxwell width), now explicitly stated to constrain the process and not the mechanism, and not to be distinguishable from Lamb's two-regime reading above 30 um. A two-term at-capture orientation-inheritance model (per-voxel walk `D_v` plus an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck-correlated per-grain rotation-vector walk `D_g`, `rho`) is implemented in both SPPARKS additive apps and a Moser-source GP700 deck ("v10") is calibrated in-sample on Lamb's Fig. 5a. The revision adds two held-out comparisons (Lamb's established-grain curve, Fig. 5c; a digitized 2026 Fig. 7a heatmap), a corrected per-grain comparison that now concedes the model accumulates at about half Lamb's average rate with far smaller extremes, a seed-scatter study (three proxy seeds per model, a second full v10 build), a hatch-periodicity quantification with a random-offset sensitivity test, a 2-degree re-segmentation, an origin census over 26 runs, a measured path factor, and a v10 resolution test at 1.5 um with a stated rescaling rule for `rho` and `D_g`. The abstract and findings have been rewritten to scale the claims to this evidence and to list the limitations.

## 2. Disposition of the earlier review's major issues

**M1 (calibration = validation) — addressed, with one overstatement.** Sec. 7.1 now labels the Fig. 5a score in-sample and says "the population curve does not choose between the two models". Sec. 7.2 / Table `tab:heldout` add the established curve and the 2026 heatmap, neither used in calibration (Sec. 6). The established test is a genuine discriminator: v10 RMS 0.28 deg / bias -0.11 deg against v8 +2.98 deg, and the v8 failure is far outside the seed scatter (Table `tab:seeds`). *Remaining gap:* the 2026 modal comparison does not support the abstract's wording that v10 "reproduces ... the modal SROD trajectory of the 2026 dataset". From Table `tab:heldout`, at 5 / 30 / 60 / 115 um the mode is Lamb 1.6 / 2.9 / 3.7 / 4.7, v10 0.8 / 1.8 / 2.8 / 4.8, v8 1.8 / 2.8 / 3.8 / 5.8. v10 is 30-50 % low over the first 60 um and only meets Lamb at 115 um; v8 tracks the first three points to within 0.2 deg and misses only the last. The RMS of the four modal residuals is about 0.8 deg for v10 and 0.6 deg for v8. The 2026 mode therefore does not favour v10, and the sentence "the modal trajectory of v10 follows Lamb's from 1.6 to 4.7 deg" (Sec. 7.2) is not what the table says (v10 starts at 0.8). Only the established curve should be claimed as a passed held-out test. Also, the second full v10 build gives an established-class score of 0.47 deg / +0.25 deg (Sec. 7.9); the abstract and Summary quote only the better of the two seeds (0.28 / -0.11).

**M2 (per-grain reproduction, mis-citation) — addressed.** The "20-24 deg max vs Lamb's 20-25 deg" sentence is gone; Table `tab:grains` now confronts the simulation with Lamb's per-layer means at 300 um, the fraction of grains above 25 deg (5 % vs "most"), the 58.7 deg maximum and the 2026 accumulation rates, and Sec. 7.4 concludes plainly that the model falls short and that the experiment has a heavy tail. The drift-to-spread ratio is now labelled as confirming the construction. *Remaining gaps:* (i) Lamb's "20-25 deg" is stated in the paper as "misorientations as large as 20-25 deg develop during >300 um" — an upper bound over G1-G4, not the range of all four; Table `tab:grains` "20-25 deg (G1-G4)" implies all four. (ii) The 2026 average rate 1.7 deg / 30 um is used in two incompatible ways: in Sec. 7.2 it is "the slope of that ridge near the start", i.e. a short-range modal slope; in Table `tab:grains` it is the population-average per-grain rate against which the model's 0.84 is "half". Choose one reading. (iii) Lamb's own per-grain numbers are mutually inconsistent (see Sec. 4 below): "on average >25 deg over 300 um" cannot coexist with "0.7-1.0 deg per layer" and with a Fig. 5a mean of ~11 deg at 260 um. The report should say which of these it treats as the target and why, rather than presenting all of them as one coherent experimental picture the model misses. (iv) "Maximum SROD in the grain" is an extreme-value statistic over ~10^5 EBSD voxels and is sensitive to misindexed and boundary voxels; the simulation has no such noise. A one-line caveat is needed before "33 deg vs 58.7 deg" is read as a model deficit.

**M3 (grain definition) — partly addressed.** The 2 deg re-segmentation is done (Sec. 7.6) and the outcome (v10: 139 filtered grains, SROD score within 0.6 deg; v8: no grain taller than 165 um) is informative. *Remaining gaps:* (i) The numbers are internally inconsistent: "139 pass Lamb's filters ... ESD p50 29 um" and "54 filtered grains of ESD p50 8 um" cannot both hold with a >= 40 um ESD filter. Either the ESD quoted is over all labels (say so) or the filter was not applied as stated. (ii) The result is then set aside ("the spin labelling is the one used elsewhere") on the ground that the simulated field carries voxel-scale noise the EBSD does not. That is a fair observation but not a resolution: it should be tested by a threshold sweep (e.g. 2, 3, 5 deg) or by a noise-matched pre-smoothing of the simulated field before segmentation, and the report should state which grain definition it regards as comparable to Lamb's. (iii) The finding that only "two grains span 300 um" under 2 deg segmentation means the 2-deg-segmented simulation is full of grains that *originate mid-build* — precisely what the origin census (Sec. 7.7) says the simulation lacks. This connection is not made (see M6).

**M4 (hatch-locked microstructure) — addressed as a diagnosis, partly as a test.** Sec. 7.5 and Fig. `fig:hatch` quantify the periodicity (autocorrelation 0.6-0.7 at the 80 um lag; 43-62 % of spectral power), give in-plane widths (median 30-51 um, p90 72-84 um), and state the cause. The random per-layer hatch offset is a reasonable sensitivity test and its result (SROD score and per-grain statistics unchanged) is the useful part. *Remaining gaps:* (i) A normalised autocorrelation of the height-integrated boundary density is phase-insensitive, so it cannot distinguish a period produced by layer-to-layer registration from one produced by the pool spacing within each layer; the mixed outcome (x: 0.46 -> 0.34; y: 0.45 -> 0.48) is therefore uninformative about registration. The right diagnostic is the cross-correlation of boundary positions between layers n and n+2, or a per-layer boundary map. (ii) The unchanged grain widths and ESD under the offset (27-36 vs 30-33 um; 120 vs 123 um) are the strongest evidence that the columnar selection is not registration-driven — the report should say so directly rather than "only partly". (iii) One realisation on the proxy. (iv) Lamb's Fig. 2f (2024) is an in-plane EBSD map from which a rough in-plane grain size could be read; "the comparison with Lamb's Fig. 2f is qualitative because they give no in-plane size" (response) is true of the text but the figure could still be used.

**M5 (seed scatter) — addressed.** Table `tab:seeds` (three proxy seeds each) and the second full v10 build (Sec. 7.9) give the scatter that was missing, and the conclusion that the in-sample v8/v10 difference is ~2 sigma and not decisive is the correct one. *Remaining gaps:* the two full builds differ by 0.28 deg in bias (-0.28 vs 0.00) and by 0.19 deg in established-class bias (-0.11 vs +0.25), i.e. more than the proxy scatter (+/-0.11); with n = 2 nothing can be said about the full-build scatter, and the abstract should quote the pair (or their mean), not the first build alone.

**M6 (grain classes, no mid-build origins) — partly addressed.** The floor-referenced comparison is now shown (Table `tab:heldout`) and the origin census (26 runs, 6.5e12-8.5e13 /m^3, t_c 3-23 K) is an honest negative. *Remaining gap:* the census counts origins of *spin-label* grains, whereas Lamb's >500 originating grains are DREAM.3D 2-deg-segmentation products; the 2024 paper itself shows a grain (G4) developing a low-angle boundary during growth, and any grain that splits at >= 2 deg produces a new "originating" grain with a mid-volume lowest point. The report's own 2 deg re-segmentation produces exactly such a population (139 filtered grains, almost all shorter than 300 um) but the origin census is never run on it. Until it is, the "5:1 against 0-7 per run" comparison is not like-for-like, and the statement "nucleated grains are overgrown at every tested density" — while probably true — is not shown to be the relevant deficiency.

**M7 (path factor asserted) — addressed.** Measured from d10: 1.47 at 3 um, 1.68 at 1.5 um (Table `tab:grains`, Sec. 7.8), and the text now separates this from the fitted-to-input D ratio. *Remaining gap:* the arithmetic that would close the loop is not shown. Per unit path the model's long-range variance rate is `D_v + D_g (1+rho)/(1-rho) = 0.065 + 0.19 = 0.255 deg^2/um`; times the path factor 1.47 this predicts `D = 0.375` against height, whereas Table `tab:v10` fits 0.43. The ~15 % surplus is presumably the ballistic (sub-correlation-length) regime of the OU walk, but the report should say where it comes from, since this is the one place the model's internal consistency can be checked.

**M8 (physical premise) — addressed.** Sec. 5 "Misorientation model" now states that the model is an at-capture proxy for what Lamb attribute to shrinkage and HAZ cycling, that it cannot represent dependence on layers deposited above, and that a 12-layer build is compared with block-interior material. *Note:* the 2026 paper (Sec. IV.C) offers a second mechanism — "torsion and deflection forces from neighboring cells during solidification in the melt pool", supported by synchrotron Laue observations of cell torsion in the pool, with shear strains 0.23-2.99 % — which is an *at-solidification* mechanism and would support the report's proxy more directly than the HAZ argument. The report cites only the shrinkage + HAZ reading and so undersells its own premise.

**Claim D (resolution independence) — partly addressed.** The v10 deck was run at 1.5 um with `rho' = 0.95`, `D_g' = 0.0049` (the rescaling rule is correct: it holds both the 30 um correlation length and the long-range rate `D_g (1+rho)/(1-rho)` fixed). *Remaining gaps:* (i) The excess is understated. Sec. 7.8 says the mean "runs 5-15 % above the 3 um value at every height" and then lists 3.4 vs 3.3 (3 %), 9.6 vs 8.7 (10 %), 12.4 vs 10.1 (23 %). The excess grows with height and reaches 23 % in the mean, i.e. ~50 % in variance. (ii) The attribution "traceable to the longer resolved growth path" is not demonstrated: the path factor rises from 1.47 to 1.68 (+14 % in path, hence +14 % in variance, +7 % in RMS), which accounts for a third of the 23 % at 282 um. (iii) The 1.5 um bias of +0.64 deg is ~5 sigma of the proxy seed scatter (+0.05 +/- 0.11). Calling this "resolution independent to first order" is generous; "a 10-25 % height-dependent excess on halving the voxel, only partly explained by the resolved path" would be accurate. The finding is still useful and the rescaling rule is a real contribution.

## 3. New concerns raised by the revision

1. **The ESD figures are physically impossible on the stated definition (affects Tables `tab:v10`, `tab:seeds`, Sec. 7.6).** The response and Sec. 6 state that grains cut by the crop are kept with their cropped volume and that ESD is computed on the cropped grain. The v10 crop is 400 x 400 x ~410 um = 6.6e7 um^3. Table `tab:v10` reports 97 filtered grains with ESD p50 = 148 um: at least 48 grains each of volume >= pi/6 x 148^3 = 1.7e6 um^3, i.e. >= 8.2e7 um^3 — more than the whole crop, before counting the other 48 grains or the unfiltered ones. The proxy (Table `tab:seeds`: 55 grains, ESD p50 123 um in 300 x 300 x ~280 um = 2.5e7 um^3; the top 27 grains alone need 2.6e7 um^3) fails the same check. The 2 deg segmentation numbers (filtered grains with ESD p50 29 and 8 um under a >= 40 um filter) point to the same problem from the other side. Either the ESD is not a volume-equivalent diameter of the cropped grain, or a unit/voxel-size factor is wrong. This matters beyond bookkeeping: the >= 40 um ESD filter selects the grain population on which every SROD curve, count and per-grain statistic is computed. The authors must state the definition actually used, verify the sum of filtered grain volumes against the crop volume, and re-run the filter if it was mis-applied.

2. **The 2026 held-out comparison (Table `tab:heldout`, Fig. `fig:heat`).** (a) The 2026 paper does not say that Fig. 7a is restricted to originating grains; it says only that "the same metric and normalization methods as in Reference 12 are used" and that distance is "from the starting point of the voxel's parent grain". In a 169 um tall volume many grains cross the floor. The digitized mean is flat at ~5 deg from 5 um onward (Fig. `fig:heat`, orange solid), which is exactly what floor-referenced established grains would contribute (Lamb 2024 Fig. 5c starts at ~5.6 deg; the report's own fitted "initial spread" is 6.2 deg RMS). This is a simpler explanation of the "secondary band at 10-15 deg present from the grain start" than the split-grain analogy offered in Sec. 7.2, and it would mean that only the mode, not the mean, is comparable to the simulated originating grains. (b) Digitizing a probability density from a colour heatmap requires inverting the colour bar; the density-weighted mean is very sensitive to how the low-density tail is mapped, and no uncertainty is given. The mode is robust; the mean should either be dropped or given an uncertainty. (c) As noted under M1, the modal numbers favour v8 over 5-60 um. (d) The 2026 mode of 1.6 deg at 5 um (0.1 um in-plane voxels, so not a resolution artefact of the 2024 data) is the sub-30 um information the first review asked for, and it is above both the `D = 0.42` walk (Maxwell mode ~1.2 deg at 5 um) and v10 (0.8 deg). It is consistent with Lamb's "sharp initial increase". The report should say what it concludes from this for Finding 1 ("the published curves do not distinguish sqrt(h) from the two-regime reading") instead of leaving it in a table.

3. **Seed-scatter table (Table `tab:seeds`).** Supported and useful. Two remarks: with n = 3 the standard deviations have ~2 degrees of freedom, so ">5 sigma in each direction" (Sec. 7.9) should be read loosely (drift: 2.7 deg difference over pooled sd ~0.6; spread: 2.8 over ~0.8); and v10's band ratio (1.53 +/- 0.07 proxy, 1.50 and 1.46 full) sits ~2 sigma above the Maxwell/Lamb value 1.38-1.4 while v8's (1.45 +/- 0.05, 1.41) does not, which slightly weakens the sentence "with Gaussian increments the population band stays at Lamb's value" (Sec. 7.1).

4. **Hatch-offset test (Sec. 7.5).** See M4: the metric cannot test registration; the unchanged grain size is the informative result; one seed.

5. **2-degree segmentation (Sec. 7.6).** See M3 and concern 1. Additionally, "same-grain neighbour disorientation median 2.4 deg" is the key number: it says the simulated intragranular field is noisier at the voxel scale than EBSD (2026: KAM > 1 deg is confined to 40 nm cell walls; cell interiors are uniform). This is a property of the per-voxel walk with independent lineages and single-parent inheritance, and it is worth a sentence on whether any `D_v > 0` can avoid it — if not, the model cannot be segmented like the experiment for any parameter choice.

6. **Origin census (Sec. 7.7).** Honest and clearly reported, but on the wrong grain definition for the comparison it makes (see M6). "83 originate at the layer-0 remelt floor ... and 3 above 160 um" still accounts for 86 of the "88 filtered grains" — unchanged from the first review.

7. **Resolution test (Sec. 7.8).** See Claim D. Also: `D_v` is per unit path and was not rescaled; if the excess is a path-resolution effect, the natural test is to rescale `D_v` (and `D_g`) by the measured path-factor ratio (1.47/1.68) and show that the bias vanishes. That would turn "a fit at the target resolution would absorb" into a demonstration.

8. **Established-class test (Sec. 7.2).** This is now the report's key discriminating result, so its sensitivity should be shown. In the model the intragranular spread of a grain grows with path from its origin (independent lineages diverge), so the floor-referenced SROD at 25 um depends on how far above the origin the floor is placed (240 um here, i.e. ~140 um of growth); Lamb's established grains have grown for hundreds of layers. A floor at 200 and 300 um would show whether the 0.28 deg agreement is robust or a coincidence of the floor choice. The Lamb established band ratio in Table `tab:heldout` ("1.4 (2024, above 100 um: 1.1-1.25)") should be the 25-233 um average of the digitized curve (which falls from ~1.8 to ~1.15 over that range and does average near 1.4), and "above 100 um" contradicts "beyond 150 um" in Sec. 3.

## 6. Questions for the authors (still open)

1. What ESD definition produced p50 = 148 um for 97 grains in a 6.6e7 um^3 crop, and 29 / 8 um for "filtered" 2-deg grains under a >= 40 um filter? Does the sum of filtered grain volumes fit in the crop? Was the 40 um filter applied on the same quantity?
2. How were `D_v`, `D_g` and `rho` chosen, and was Fig. 5c (established curve) or any intragranular-spread target consulted while choosing the `D_v : D_g` split? If the split was tuned to make the intragranular spread "small", the established test is partly in-sample by intent.
3. Does the 2026 Fig. 7a include floor-crossing (established) grains? If the paper does not say, why is the flat ~5 deg mean from 5 um not simply their floor offset? How was the heatmap colour scale inverted to a density, and what is the uncertainty on the digitized mean?
4. Given the modal residuals in Table `tab:heldout` (v8 closer over 5-60 um, v10 closer at 115 um), on what basis is the 2026 mode claimed as a v10 success?
5. How does the established-class agreement change with the floor placed at 200 and 300 um instead of 240 um?
6. Resolution: why does the excess grow with height (3 %, 10 %, 23 %)? Does rescaling `D_v` and `D_g` by (1.47/1.68) remove the +0.64 deg bias?
7. What does `(D_v + D_g (1+rho)/(1-rho)) x 1.47 = 0.375` versus the fitted 0.43 tell you about the OU ballistic regime, and does the 2-4 us timestep or smoothing contribute?
8. Origin census on the 2-deg-segmented volume: how many 2-deg grains originate > 60 um above the floor, and what is their originating:established ratio?
9. Threshold sweep for segmentation (2, 3, 5 deg): at what threshold do the simulated grain counts and ESD match the spin labels, and how does that threshold compare with the simulated same-grain neighbour disorientation (2.4 deg median)?
10. Which of Lamb's mutually inconsistent per-grain numbers (Fig. 5a mean ~11 deg at 260 um; 0.7-1.0 deg/layer; "on average >25 deg over 300 um"; 2026 1.7 deg / 30 um) is the intended target for the "half Lamb's rate" conclusion?

## 7. Overall recommendation: **minor revision**

The revision is substantial, responsive and candid: every major issue was acted on, the calibration is labelled in-sample, a real held-out discriminator (the established-grain curve) has been added and it separates the two models far outside the seed scatter, the per-grain comparison now confronts Lamb's actual numbers and concedes the shortfall, seed scatter and hatch periodicity are quantified, the path factor is measured, the resolution test is run on the right deck with a defensible rescaling rule, and the abstract lists the limitations. The remaining problems are of two kinds. One is a possible correctness problem that must be resolved before the tables can be trusted: the reported ESD values are impossible on the stated definition, and since the >= 40 um ESD filter defines the grain population behind every result, the authors must state the definition actually used and confirm the filter. If that check reveals a mis-applied filter, the grain tables and possibly the SROD curves must be recomputed and the recommendation becomes major revision.
